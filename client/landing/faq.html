FAQ

Clear answers before you start.

If your question is not here, the privacy statement and About page may help.

The basics

Is this a distributor?

No. You still release through a distributor of your choice. Track Record helps you prepare accurate metadata, agree splits and register your compositions, which a distributor does not do.

What is the difference between a distributor and a society?

A distributor delivers your recording to streaming services and pays recording (master) royalties. A collecting society collects royalties on the composition, such as radio, venues and streaming performances, for its members.

Do I need an ISRC or UPC before I start?

No. Distributors commonly issue these after you upload, and a society issues an ISWC once a work is registered. Leave them blank and add them later.

Is it free?

Creating an account is free. Track Record is currently a pilot, so details may change as we learn from the artists using it.

Which countries does it cover?

Registration guides cover Rwanda, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Burundi, DR Congo and South Sudan. Pick your country to see which society applies, what its status is and the steps to register.

Releases and splits

Do my co-writers need accounts?

No. They receive an emailed link to confirm their share. If they later sign up with the same verified email, the song appears in their Collaborations.

What does Ready mean?

A release can be marked ready when it passes the readiness check: among other things, a release date, at least one song, an audio file on every song and splits that total 100%. Ready releases are locked so the details you hand to your distributor do not change by accident. You can move one back to draft unless a dispute is open.

What happens if a co-writer disagrees with their share?

They can dispute the split or remove themselves from the credit from their link. You see every answer in one place and can resolve it from the splits page.

Can the app register my songs with a society for me?

Not yet. Societies use their own portals, so the app prepares what you need and explains the steps. Always confirm requirements with the society before paying or signing anything.

Is the registration information always up to date?

We date every country guide and link our sources, but this sector changes fast. Treat the guide as a starting point and confirm with the body itself.

Performance

Where do the performance numbers come from?

You import them. Download a CSV of your stream counts from your distributor or a streaming dashboard such as Spotify for Artists, then import it in the Performance page. Track Record shows you how it reads each column before anything is saved. It does not log in to your streaming accounts.

Which platforms can I look at?

Any platform that appears in your export. Common ones are Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, Audiomack, Boomplay, Deezer, SoundCloud, Tidal and Amazon Music. Once your numbers are in, you choose which platforms and songs to show.

How are streams matched to my songs?

By ISRC. Rows whose ISRC does not match one of your songs are counted and reported to you as unmatched rather than silently dropped, so add the missing ISRC to the song and import again.

Can I download a report?

Yes. You can export a PDF for a single month, a full year or a custom date range, for everything or for just the platforms and songs you have selected.

The Chrome extension

What does the extension do?

The Track Record helper copies a ready release into a distributor's upload form: text boxes, drop-downs, yes or no answers and artist rows. You check everything and press submit yourself.

What does it never do?

It never submits a form, ticks terms, uploads files, or touches login, payment or bank fields. Nothing runs until you press a button, and there is no background script.

How do I install it?

It is not on the Chrome Web Store yet. Get it from the extension page (it links to the extension folder on GitHub, so it is always the latest version), download the repository as a zip and unzip it, open chrome://extensions, switch on Developer mode, choose Load unpacked and pick the extension folder. Then open it and sign in.

Which distributors does it work with?

It is built around the field names that RouteNote asks for and has been tested on a practice page that copies that layout. DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby and Amuse are listed but switched off by default, and their real forms have not been verified. Anything the helper does not recognise is skipped and listed, and every value has a Copy button.

What can the extension see?

Only the page you are on, and only when you press a button (the activeTab permission). It has no access to the rest of your browsing and sends no telemetry. Your sign-in token is kept in session storage and dropped when the browser closes.

Does it work in other browsers?

It is built for Chrome 108 or newer. Other Chromium-based browsers may accept it, but we have not tested them.

Your data

Who can see my releases?

Only you, plus the co-writers you credit, who see the song title, their own credit and the split you entered. Read the full privacy statement.

Can I download or delete my data?

Yes. In Settings you can download your data and delete your account. Releases marked ready or released must be moved back to draft and deleted first.
