About us

Built so no artist is left guessing.

Track Record is a release tool for independent artists in Rwanda and East Africa, made to turn the paperwork of music into something you can understand and finish.

The problem

The metadata gap

Independent artists often release music with incomplete or inconsistent details: missing ISRC and UPC codes, unclear songwriter and publisher splits, credits that do not match from one place to the next. The cost arrives later, as rejected distributor uploads, royalties that never reach the right person, and ownership disputes that are hard to settle once a song is out.

Most of this is not carelessness. Nobody explains what the fields mean, who needs them, or what happens when they are wrong.

Learn by doing. Every release you prepare should teach you something you will use on the next one.

What we built

Track Record is a guided release tool. You prepare a release with plain-language help on every field, agree splits with your co-writers before the song goes out, watch how your songs perform on the platforms you choose, and finish knowing which collecting society to register with in your country.

It started as a capstone project, Closing the Metadata Gap, at African Leadership University (ALU) in Kigali, Rwanda, working at the meeting point of software and Africa's creative industries. It is currently a pilot, and it is shaped by the artists who test it.

What we believe

Three ideas behind the tool

01

Clarity over jargon

Royalties, rights and identifiers are explained where you meet them, in words an artist would use, not buried in a glossary you never open.

02

Collaborators are partners

Producers and co-writers confirm their share with a link in their email, no account needed. Agreements are made before release, not argued about after.

03

Your catalogue stays yours

You can download your data and delete your account at any time. We do not sell your data or use it for advertising.

Honest limits

What Track Record is not

Track Record is not a distributor, so you keep releasing through the one you choose. It is not a collecting society, so it cannot collect royalties for you. It does not register songs on your behalf, because societies use their own portals; it prepares what you need and explains the steps. And it is general guidance, not legal or financial advice, so always confirm requirements with your distributor and society.

Where something is uncertain, such as which society is operating in a country today, we say so, date the information and link our sources.
